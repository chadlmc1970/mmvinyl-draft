Press guide

Pressing on polyester, jerseys and performance wear

Poly jerseys and dry fit tees take a print just fine. They just need less heat, a test press, and a few tricks cotton never asks for. Here is how to get a clean print without pink whites or shiny boxes.

  • About 6 minutes
  • Maker settings, cited
  • Printable test-press log
Shop jerseys

Before you start

Have these ready

  • One spare blank for a test press. Same style and same color as the run. A test on a white shirt tells you nothing about a red one.
  • The press settings for your film. See the UltraColor MAX guide or the Siser Easy Transfers guide.
  • A cover sheet. Teflon or parchment, big enough to cover the print and then some.
  • The test-press log at the bottom of this page, printed out. It takes a minute to fill in and saves you from guessing next season.

Jump to a step

  1. Know your fabric
  2. Turn the heat down
  3. Watch for dye migration
  4. Stop shiny box marks
  5. Press mesh jerseys
  6. Nylon and coated jackets
  7. Run a test press

1Step 1Know your fabric

Read the tag before you set the press. "Performance," "dry fit," and "moisture wicking" almost always mean polyester. These are the polyester blanks we carry:

  • Sport-Tek Replica Jersey and the women's Replica Jersey. SanMar, Sport-Tek's distributor, lists the replica jersey line as a 100% polyester mesh body.
  • Performance Tee and youth Performance Tee, 100% polyester.
  • Sport-Tek Ladies Racerback Tank, 100% polyester interlock.
  • Jerzees DRI-POWER 50/50 is half polyester, so treat it with the same care.

Good to know

Stahl's lists cotton, polyester, cotton/poly blends, and tri-blends as fabrics for UltraColor MAX. Polyester is on the list. It just runs cooler.

2Step 2Turn the heat down

Polyester is heat sensitive. Too much heat can scorch it, leave marks, and pull dye out of the fabric. Both films we print list 290°F, and Stahl's tells you to use that lower number for heat-sensitive fabrics like polyester.

Do not go hotter to fix a lift

The cotton setting on the UltraColor MAX page is higher. Do not borrow it for polyester. If an edge lifts, check pressure and time first, then call us.

3Step 3Watch for dye migration

Dye migration is when the color of the shirt creeps up into your print. A white logo on a red or maroon jersey slowly turns pink. Heat is what lets the dye move, which is one more reason to keep polyester at the lower setting.

Not a dye blocker

Stahl's says UltraColor MAX "does not eliminate or prevent dye migration, or serve as a dye blocker." Lower heat helps, but it is not a guarantee.

  • Test first. Press one spare at the maker setting, let it cool, and look again later. Write what you see on the log card.
  • Light print on a dark poly shirt? That is the risky combination. A dark print on a light shirt is the safe one.
  • Seeing pink in the test? Stop before the full run. The job needs a dye-blocking product, so call us at (225) 478-9319 and we will talk through your options.

4Step 4Stop shiny box marks

On smooth polyester, the edge of the top platen can leave a shiny or white square around the print. It is the fabric's surface getting flattened, not the transfer.

  • Stay at the maker temperature. Extra heat makes marks worse.
  • Lift the print area. Put a pressing pillow, a mouse pad, or a folded cotton tee under the print inside the shirt. The print sits higher than the rest, so the platen edge does not bite.
  • Use a cover sheet over the whole area for any second press.
  • Do not over-squeeze. Medium pressure is the maker setting for UltraColor MAX.

The lift and cover-sheet tips are common shop practice, not maker specs.

5Step 5Press mesh jerseys

Mesh has holes. A transfer only bonds where there is fabric, so the glue can push through the holes and stick the front of the jersey to the back. Transfer Express, a Stahl's company, gives two fixes.

  1. Cover sheet between the layers

    Slide a cover sheet inside the jersey, under the print area. Press as normal. The sheet keeps front and back apart.

  2. Or thread one layer

    Pull the jersey onto the lower platen like a pillowcase so only the front layer is under the transfer.

Source: Transfer Express, decorating football apparel. Sizing names and numbers for a team? The same page lists 8 inch front and 10 inch back numbers for adult football jerseys.

6Step 6Nylon and coated jackets

Windbreakers, rain jackets, and many bags are nylon or have a coating. Neither UltraColor MAX nor Siser Easy Transfers lists nylon as a fabric. Settings you find online for nylon do not agree with each other, so we do not publish one.

Ask us first

Pressing on nylon or a coated jacket? Call us at (225) 478-9319 or email sales@mandmvinyl.com before you order. Tell us the brand and style of the jacket and we will tell you what we know.

7Step 7Run a test press on every new blank

Every new style or color gets one test before the run. It costs one shirt and saves a whole order.

  1. Pre-press the spare

    A few seconds to flatten it and drive out moisture. The UltraColor MAX guide lists 3 to 5 seconds.

  2. Press at the maker setting

    290°F for both films we print. Lift the print area if the shirt marks easily.

  3. Peel and look

    Check the edges, the shine around the print, and the color of any white or light ink.

  4. Look again later

    Check the whites again after the shirt has sat for a while, and after one wash if you can.

  5. Write it down

    Fill in a line on the log card. Next time you get the same blank, the answer is already on paper.

  • Tag says polyester? Press at 290°F
  • Red or dark poly? Test the whites first
  • Print area lifted, no box marks
  • Mesh? Cover sheet inside the jersey
  • Nylon? Call us before you order

Good to know

Order a spare or two of every blank with a team order. Our gang sheet guide shows how to fit extra test prints into the empty space on your sheet.
