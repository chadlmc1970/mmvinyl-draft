How to apply patches

Sequin, chenille, puff, and rhinestone patches all go on with heat. Here is how to press or iron each one so it stays put, plus a card for each type you can print and keep by the press.

  • 4 patch types
  • Heat press or home iron
  • Printable card
Shop patches

Before you start

Have these ready

  • Your patch and the garment. Cotton, polyester, or a cotton and poly blend works best. Our crewneck sweatshirts and hoodies are a 50/50 blend.
  • A heat press or a home iron. Either one works. No press? The home iron guide shows how to press by hand.
  • A cover sheet. Parchment paper or a thin cotton pressing cloth, so heat never hits sequins or stones directly.
  • Heat Transfer Application Tape. A strip or two holds the patch still while you press.
  • The heat and time for your patch. It depends on who made it. Call (225) 478-9319 and we will tell you.

1Step 1Know your patch

Every patch we sell has a heat activated glue on the back. The steps are the same for all four types. What changes is how careful you need to be with the front.

Sequin
Flat plastic sequins stitched onto a backing. Direct heat can curl or dull them, so always use a cover sheet. Smooth them all one way before you press. Try the Saints, Game Day, Fleur De Lis, or Mardi Gras Bow patches.
Chenille
Thick looped yarn, like a letterman jacket letter. It is the thickest patch, so heat has to travel through it. Pressing from the back matters most here. See the Cheer and God is Good chenille patches.
Puff embroidered
Raised stitching over a foam layer. Press firmly enough to bond, but do not crush it flat. A pressing pillow or folded towel under the shirt helps. See the Puff Mascot patch.
Rhinestone
An embroidered patch with glued on stones. Never put an iron straight on the stones. Cover them, and do most of the work from the back. See the Jaguars rhinestone patch.
Ask us for the setting

Heat and time depend on who made the patch, so we do not post one number for all of them. Call (225) 478-9319 or email sales@mandmvinyl.com with the patch name and we will give you the setting for that patch.

What a maker sheet covers

Stahls publishes a full sheet for its own chenille patches: time, temperature, and pressure for three kinds of press, a pre-press, the fabrics it works on, and wash care. That is the level of detail to ask for. Our patches may come from a different maker, so check with us before you use another maker's numbers.

2Step 2Pick the spot

Lay the garment flat and try the patch in place before you heat anything. To find the center, fold the shirt in half from shoulder to hem and pinch a crease down the middle. Line the center of the patch up with the crease.

  1. Center chest. The classic spot for a big sequin or chenille patch on a Christmas sweatshirt.
  2. Left chest. On the wearer's left, so it is on your right as you look at the shirt. Good for small patches.
  3. Sleeve. Small patches only. Slide a folded towel inside the sleeve so you press one layer.
  4. Tote. Center it a little above the middle so it shows when the bag is full.
  5. Belt bag. Center it on the front panel, like on our Mardi Gras Belt Bag. Check the fabric first (see the next step).

Hats

A flat press cannot reach a curved hat front well. A cap press does it best, and Stahls lists a cap press setting for its chenille patches. If you want to put one of our patches on a hat, call us first and we will talk you through it.

3Step 3Prep the garment

  1. Pre-press it

    Press the bare garment for a few seconds. This dries out moisture and flattens wrinkles, so the glue sticks to fabric, not to steam.

  2. Lint roll the spot

    Fuzz and lint under the patch stop the glue from reaching the fabric.

  3. Place and tape

    Set the patch on the crease and hold it with a strip of heat transfer tape so it cannot slide.

Check the fabric

Stahls' chenille sheet lists cotton, polyester, and cotton and poly blends, and warns against heat sensitive fabrics such as some nylons, silk, leather, wool, and fleece. Most sweatshirts are a cotton and poly blend. Polyester polar fleece, puffer jackets, nylon bags, and anything slick or rubbery: call us before you press.

4Step 4Press the front

Lay your cover sheet over the patch and press. On a heat press, use medium pressure. Stahls lists medium for its chenille. With a home iron, turn the steam off, press straight down with firm pressure, and hold still. Do not slide the iron.

Good to know

A big patch is wider than an iron. Press one half, lift, then press the other half, overlapping a little in the middle.

5Step 5Press from the back

Turn the garment inside out and press again on the back of the patch area. Heat from the back melts the glue into the fabric without cooking the front. This is the step people skip, and it is the one that keeps edges down. It matters most on thick chenille and on rhinestone patches.

Put a pad or folded towel under the patch for this press, so sequins, puff, and stones sink in instead of getting crushed.

6Step 6Let it cool

Leave the garment flat until the patch is cool to the touch. The glue sets as it cools. Bending, stretching, or peeling at a warm edge pulls it loose before it has a chance to grab.

Once it is cool, run a fingernail around the edge. It should feel sealed all the way around.

7Step 7Fix a lifted edge

  1. Cover and press again

    Cover sheet on top, press the front of the lifted area, then turn it inside out and press the back.

  2. Hold it down while it cools

    Press the edge down with something flat and cool, like a book, until it sets.

  3. Still lifting?

    A few stitches around the edge will hold any patch for good. Or call us and tell us the patch and the fabric.

8Step 8Wash and care

Stahls' chenille sheet says to wash cold, inside out, with a mild detergent, and hang to dry. That is a safe habit for every patch type.

  • Turn it inside out
  • Wash cold with a mild detergent
  • Hang to dry
  • Never iron straight on the front of the patch

9PrintablePrint a patch card

One card per patch type, four to a page. Pick the types you want, print, and cut along the dashed lines. Tuck one in with every patch order, or keep one by your press. Write the setting we give you in the blank.

Check before you wear it

  • The garment is cotton, polyester, or a blend
  • A cover sheet was over the front every time
  • You pressed from the back too
  • It cooled fully before you handled it
  • A fingernail finds no loose edge
