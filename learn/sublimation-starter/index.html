Sublimation starter: shirts, mugs, tumblers

Sublimation turns ink into part of the blank, so it never peels or cracks. It only works on the right blanks, and it needs your own sublimation printer. Here is how to get bright results on shirts, mugs, and tumblers.

  • Shirts, mugs, tumblers

Have these ready

  • Sublimation paper. We stock TexPrintR Sublimation Paper, by the sheet or the pack.

1Step 1How sublimation works

Heat turns sublimation ink into a gas, and the gas soaks into polyester fibers or a polyester coating. When it cools, the color is inside the blank, not sitting on top. That is why it never cracks or peels, and why you cannot feel it.

Sublimation ink is also see-through. There is no white ink, so the blank's own color shows through every part of the design.

2Step 2Pick the right blank

Sublimation needs polyester or a polyester coating, in a light color. Sawgrass, a sublimation ink maker, says more polyester gives brighter color that lasts longer. Cotton will not hold it. A cotton or 50/50 shirt is a job for a DTF gang sheet instead.

Blanks we stock for sublimation

  • Shirts: Sublimation Tee (100% poly), youth, toddler, and ladies V-neck
  • Tank: Racerback Tank for Sublimation, 100% polyester
  • Drinkware: 11 oz Ceramic Mug (pickup only) and 20 oz Skinny Tumbler
  • Soft goods: 9 Panel Blanket, Handkerchief, and more in the sublimation shop

Dark shirts and cotton

Sublimation will not show on black or dark shirts, and it washes out of cotton. For those, order a DTF gang sheet and press it at home.

3Step 3Print it mirrored

The paper goes face down on the blank, so the print has to be backwards. Turn on mirror (sometimes called flip horizontal) in your printer settings. Print on the bright white printable side of the paper.

Good to know

The print looks dull on the paper. The color brightens when it is pressed, so do not judge it until after the press.

4Step 4Tape it down

If the paper shifts even a little while it is hot, you get a blurry double image called ghosting. Trim the paper close to the design, set it face down, and tape the corners with Heat Transfer Application Tape. Our tape's listing puts it simply: it prevents ghosting caused by paper shift.

When you lift the paper after pressing, lift it straight up in one move. Do not drag it across the blank.

5Step 5Press a shirt

  1. Lint roll and pre-press

    Lint and moisture both cause spots. Lint roll the shirt, then press it bare for a few seconds.

  2. Paper inside the shirt

    Slide a sheet of plain paper inside so ink cannot go through to the back.

  3. Place, tape, and cover

    Print face down, corners taped, and a clean sheet of paper on top to protect the press.

  4. Press and lift

    Press for the time on your settings card. Lift the paper straight up right away.

6Step 6Press a mug

Our 11 oz Ceramic Mug has a glossy sublimation surface and fits any standard mug press.

  1. Size and trim

    Size the design to the printable area between the handle and the rim. Trim the paper so it wraps without overlapping the handle.

  2. Wrap it tight

    Print side against the mug. Tape both ends and the top and bottom edges so the paper hugs the surface with no gaps.

  3. Press

    Close the mug press and run it for the time on your settings card.

  4. Unwrap and cool

    The mug is very hot. Use heat resistant gloves, lift the paper off straight, and let the mug cool on a heat safe surface.

Ask us

Mug heat and time come from the mug supplier. Call (225) 478-9319 and we will give you the setting for our mug.

7Step 7Tumbler in shrink wrap

A 20 oz Skinny Tumbler is too long for most mug presses. Sublimation Shrink Wrap Film holds the paper tight all the way around so you can bake it instead. Our film's listing notes a heat gun is required.

  1. Wrap the paper

    Print a full wrap, mirrored. Wrap it around the tumbler print side in, and tape the seam.

  2. Slide on the sleeve

    Pull a shrink wrap sleeve over the paper so it covers the whole print.

  3. Shrink it with a heat gun

    Move the heat gun around the tumbler until the sleeve shrinks snug against the paper with no loose spots.

  4. Bake, then unwrap

    Bake in a sublimation or convection oven for the time on your settings card. Use gloves, cut off the sleeve, and peel the paper.

Ask us

Oven heat and time depend on the tumbler and the oven. Call (225) 478-9319 or email sales@mandmvinyl.com for ours.

8Step 8Why is it faded?

A faded mug or a washed out shirt almost always comes down to one of these.

Not enough heat or time
The ink did not fully turn to gas. Check the setting for your blank, and let the press or oven fully heat up first.
Not enough polyester
Cotton blends and uncoated items fade. Use a sublimation blank.
Light pressure or a loose wrap
Gaps between paper and blank print soft or blotchy. Tighten the mug press, or wrap and tape tighter.
Printed on the wrong side
Print on the bright white side of the paper.
Moisture
Damp blanks and damp paper print dull. Pre-press shirts, and keep paper sealed and dry.
Blurry double image
That is ghosting from the paper moving. Tape every corner and lift the paper straight up.

Still stuck? Email a photo to sales@mandmvinyl.com with the blank, your settings, and your printer, and we will take a look.
