Why did my transfer peel, crack, or not stick?

Most problems come down to heat, pressure, moisture, or washing too soon. Tell us when it went wrong and what it looks like, and we will point you to the fix.

  • About 3 minutes
  • UltraColor MAX and Siser Easy Transfers

Before you start

Have these ready

  • The shirt that failed. Look at it in good light. Where it failed tells you a lot.
  • Which film you pressed. UltraColor MAX or Siser Easy Transfers. It is on your order. Each has its own maker settings.
  • The settings you used. Temperature, time, pressure, and whether you peeled hot or cold.

2Step 2Know when it failed

The moment it went wrong narrows the cause fast.

At the press
A shiny box, a sticky outline, or a double image. Usually too much pressure, too much heat, or the transfer moved.
When you peeled
Ink comes up with the film, edges lift, or the film tears. Usually the real heat or pressure was low, or the peel was slow.
After the first wash
Peeling, cracking, or wrinkling. Usually under-pressed, a damp garment, or washed too soon or too hot.
After many washes
Edges lifting or cracks spreading. Usually hot dryers, harsh wash habits, or a press that was a little low from the start.

3Step 3Every symptom and its fix

The finder pulls from this list. Read it straight through if you like.

Ink comes up with the film

As you peel, part of the design stays on the film instead of the shirt.

Likely cause: the real heat at the platen is lower than the dial says, the pressure is light, the time was short, the garment was damp, or the fabric is coated or water resistant.

Fix: lay the film back down while it is still warm and press again. Then check your real heat with an infrared thermometer, pre-press the garment, and match the maker settings for your film. Coated or water-resistant fabric is not on Stahl's fabric list for UltraColor MAX, so ask us before you press it.

Edges or corners lift

The outline or the corners come up. You can catch a fingernail under them.

Likely cause: the edges got less pressure than the middle. A seam, collar, or zipper under the edge holds the platen up. Thin points and fine lines lift first.

Fix: re-press with a cover sheet. Raise the print area on a pressing pillow so seams sit lower than the design. Check that pressure is even across the platen.

The center sticks, the edges do not

The middle of the design is bonded, but a band around the outside lifts with the film.

Likely cause: the platen is cooler or lighter at its edges, or the design is bigger than the platen.

Fix: check the heat in each corner with an infrared thermometer. To check pressure, slide a strip of paper under each corner, close the press, and tug. They should all grip the same. If the design is wider than your platen, press it in sections with a cover sheet.

The film tears when you peel

The film rips partway and leaves strips on the shirt.

Likely cause: the film cooled before you peeled, or you peeled slowly or in jerks. Both of our films are hot peel.

Fix: peel right away, in one smooth motion, low and close to the shirt. If it cools on you, close the press for a few seconds to warm it, then peel.

A double image or blurry edge

A faint second copy sits beside the design, or the edges look smeared.

Likely cause: the transfer or the shirt moved while the press opened, or the film dragged across the design during the peel.

Fix: hold the transfer in place with heat transfer application tape. Open the press smoothly. Peel straight back without sliding the film.

A shiny box around the design

A shiny or darker square shows the shape of the platen or the film.

Likely cause: heavy pressure or high heat on a heat-sensitive fabric, most often polyester.

Fix: use the lower temperature your film lists. Stahl's advises 290°F for heat-sensitive fabrics like polyester. Ease the pressure to medium, and put a pressing pillow under the print area so the platen edge does not mark the shirt.

A gummy or shiny outline

A clear, tacky halo shows around the edge of the design.

Likely cause: too much heat, pressure, or time pushed the adhesive out past the ink.

Fix: stay at the maker settings for your film. Do not add extra time to be safe. If it happens at the maker settings, send us a photo.

It peels off in the wash

Large parts of the design come away after washing.

Likely cause: it was under-pressed, the garment held moisture, or it was washed too soon, too hot, or with fabric softener.

Fix: if most of it is still on, re-press with a cover sheet. For the next run, pre-press, check your real heat, and hand your customers a care card so they wash it cold.

Cracking

Fine cracks run across the print, often where the shirt stretches.

Likely cause: the ink did not fully bond (low real heat, light pressure, or short time), the shirt was stretched while hot, or it went through a hot dryer.

Fix: press at the full maker settings and let the shirt cool before you stretch or fold it. Stahl's says to machine wash cold, then tumble dry low or hang dry.

Wrinkled or puckered after washing

The print looks wavy or crinkled once the shirt is washed and dried.

Likely cause: the shirt shrank under the print, often because it held moisture or was not pre-pressed.

Fix: a short re-press with a cover sheet often flattens it. For the next run, pre-press every blank to drive out moisture and pre-shrink it.

Where these fixes come from

Re-pressing with a cover sheet, the paper-strip pressure check, and pressing pillows are common retailer guidance from Ninja Transfers and DTF Dallas. Neither Stahl's nor Siser publishes a re-press step for these films. If you are not sure, ask us before you re-press a whole order.

4Step 4The five fixes that solve most of it

  1. Pre-press the blank

    Press the empty garment first to flatten it and drive out moisture. For UltraColor MAX, the Transfer Express product guide says 3 to 5 seconds.

  2. Check the real heat

    Press dials drift. Point an infrared thermometer at the platen in the center and each corner. DTF Dallas recommends this check. If the platen reads low, raise the dial until it reads the maker's number.

  3. Re-press with a cover sheet

    For lifted edges or wrinkles, lay parchment or a cover sheet over the design and press again briefly. This is retailer guidance, so see the note above.

  4. Lift the print off the seams

    Seams, collars, zippers, and hoodie pockets hold the platen off the design. Put a pressing pillow or folded towel inside the shirt, under the print area only.

  5. Wait before the first wash

    Waiting 24 hours, then washing inside out in cold water, is common retailer advice, not a maker's spec. Our DTF care card says the same, so you can hand it to customers.

Good to know

Start with a blank that is right for the film. Stahl's lists cotton, polyester, blends, and tri-blends for UltraColor MAX. Browse our blanks, and see settings by fabric for why the fabric changes the setting.

6Step 6Press-side checklist

Print this one page and keep it by your press. Run it before each new batch.

M&M Vinyl, Baton Rouge

Before you press

  • Right film, right settings. UltraColor MAX: 290°F (325°F for 100% cotton), 12 to 15 s, medium, hot peel. Siser Easy Transfers: 290°F, 12 to 15 s, medium, hot peel.
  • Real heat checked. Infrared thermometer on the center and all four corners.
  • Pressure even. Paper strips under each corner all grip the same.
  • Fabric checked. Cotton, polyester, or a blend. Coated, nylon, or water resistant: ask us first.
  • Blank pre-pressed. Flat, dry, and lint free.
  • Seams off the print. Pillow or folded towel under the print area only.
  • Transfer placed and taped. Print side down, straight, held with heat tape.

After you press

  • Peel hot. Right away, one smooth motion, low and close to the shirt.
  • Ink stayed on the film? Lay it back down and press again before it cools.
  • Let it cool. No stretching or folding while hot.
  • Care card in the bag. Wait 24 hours, wash cold, inside out (retailer advice).
