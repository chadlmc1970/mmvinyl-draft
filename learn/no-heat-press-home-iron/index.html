No heat press? Use a home iron or EasyPress

You do not need a heat press to make a gift shirt. Some of what we sell goes on with a plain home iron or a Cricut EasyPress. Here is what works, how to do it, and a card to keep by the ironing spot.

  • Kitchen counter friendly
  • Easy Transfers, EasyWeed, patches
  • Printable card

Before you start

Have these ready

  • Know what you are applying. Siser Easy Transfers DTF, Siser EasyWeed vinyl, or a patch. Step 1 shows what works with each tool. Not sure which DTF film your sheet printed on? Ask us before you iron.
  • A hard, flat surface. A sturdy table or kitchen counter at a height where you can lean on it. Not an ironing board.
  • A home iron or EasyPress. Steam off and the water tank empty.
  • A shirt. 100% cotton like the Bella+Canvas 3001 is the easiest place to start.
  • Parchment paper. A sheet to lay over the design for the final press, and over any patch.

Why an iron needs longer

A home iron dial does not show degrees, and it has steam holes and a smaller plate. That is why the iron method holds each spot longer than a heat press and works in sections.

2Step 2Set up your surface

The iron supplies heat. You supply the pressure. An ironing board bends and soaks up both. Use a hard, flat table or counter, low enough that you can lean your weight straight down. Siser says the same for an EasyPress: a low, sturdy surface.

Lay a folded cotton towel or pillowcase on the counter only if the surface is hard enough to scratch. Keep it thin and flat.

3Step 3Heat up and prep the shirt

  1. Set the iron

    Cotton or linen setting, steam off, tank empty. Give it a few minutes to reach full heat.

  2. Press the bare shirt

    Iron the spot where the design goes. It dries out moisture and flattens wrinkles so the transfer lies flat.

  3. Place the transfer

    Fold the shirt in half to find the center and pinch a crease. Lay the transfer ink side down on the crease. A strip of heat transfer tape stops it sliding.

4Step 4Press in sections

Most designs are bigger than an iron. Press one spot at a time. Press straight down with firm pressure, lean your weight in, hold, then lift the iron and set it down on the next spot. Never slide it, or the transfer can shift and smear.

How long per spot

Ninja Transfers suggests 20 to 30 seconds per spot with firm pressure for DTF with a home iron. That is retailer guidance, not a maker spec. Use the iron's flat sole, not the pointed tip.

5Step 5Peel

Siser says to peel Easy Transfers hot. Right after the last spot, lift a corner of the film and peel it back low and slow in one smooth motion.

If any ink starts to come up with the film, stop. Lay the film back down, press that spot again, and try once more.

Vinyl and patches are different

EasyWeed vinyl has its own peel rule from Siser, and patches have no film at all. Ask us for your product if you are not sure.

6Step 6Final press and fixes

  1. Cover and press once more

    Lay parchment over the whole design and press it all again. Ninja Transfers suggests a final 15 second press (retailer guidance).

  2. Check the edges

    Once it cools, run a fingernail around the edges. Everything should feel flat and sealed.

  3. Re-press a lifted spot

    Cover it with parchment and press just that spot with firm pressure. Let it cool before you check it again.

For washing, see the DTF care card.

7Step 7Using a Cricut EasyPress

An EasyPress shows the temperature, so it takes some of the guessing out. Siser's rule is to set it about 30°F higher than the heat press setting. For Siser EasyWeed, that is 335°F.

  • Use a low, sturdy surface so you can press down.
  • Same section by section method as an iron if the design is bigger than the plate.
  • EasyWeed: mirror the design and cut with the shiny side down. Get the EasyWeed or Pattern HTV and a weeding pen.
  • Pattern HTV goes on with a Heat Transfer Mask. Ask us for its setting.

9Step 9When to get a heat press

An iron is fine for a few gifts. A heat press gives the same heat and pressure across the whole design every time. It may be time to look at one when:

  • You press more than a few shirts a week
  • Your designs are much bigger than the iron
  • Edges keep lifting even when you follow every step
  • You are selling what you make

Once you have one, start with the Siser Easy Transfers settings and the UltraColor MAX settings.

Check before you call it done

  • Your product is a "Yes" for your tool in step 1, or we told you the setting
  • You pressed on a hard surface, steam off
  • Every spot got a full, still press
  • You did a final covered press
  • No edge lifts under a fingernail
